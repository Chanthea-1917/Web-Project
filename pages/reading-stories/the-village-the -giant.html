The village and the giant

Long ago, nestled between rolling hills and a sparkling river, there was a small village called Meadowbrook. The villagers lived simple lives—growing crops, raising animals, and sharing everything with one another. Life was peaceful. But beyond the hills, in the dark Misty Mountains, there lived a giant. His name was Grumbar, and he was as tall as a castle tower. His footsteps shook the ground, and his voice was like thunder. Though he was not cruel by nature, the villagers feared him deeply. One stormy night, Grumbar came down from the mountains. The villagers saw him striding across the fields, and panic spread. Doors slammed shut, windows were barred, and not a soul dared to step outside. The next morning, they saw the damage. Grumbar had not attacked, but he had trampled their crops while searching for food. The villagers gathered in the square, their faces pale. “If he comes again, we will starve,” said the baker. “We must drive him away,” cried the blacksmith. But among them was a young girl named Elara, known for her courage and kindness. She stepped forward. “Perhaps he is not our enemy,” she said. “Perhaps he is only hungry.” The villagers shook their heads, calling her foolish, but Elara refused to stay silent. That evening, as the sun dipped below the hills, she carried a basket of bread and fruit to the edge of the fields and waited. Sure enough, the ground began to tremble—boom, boom, boom. The giant appeared, his shadow falling over her. He frowned, confused to see someone standing so boldly before him. “Why do you not run?” Grumbar rumbled. Elara’s knees shook, but she held out the basket. “Because you look hungry. Please, eat.” The giant blinked in surprise. Carefully, with fingers as big as tree trunks, he took the bread and fruit. He ate slowly, his eyes softening. “No one has ever offered me food before,” he said. “They only fear me.” Elara smiled. “Maybe because they do not know you yet.” From that night on, Elara visited Grumbar often. She brought food, and in return, he helped the village. With his great strength, he lifted heavy logs for builders, carried water from the river, and cleared rocks from the fields. The villagers, once fearful, began to see him as a friend. Children laughed as they climbed onto his shoulders, and farmers blessed him for saving their crops. One day, when a flood threatened the village, Grumbar stood in the river and built a wall of stones with his bare hands, protecting the people from disaster. From then on, no one called him a monster—they called him Guardian of Meadowbrook. The village and the giant lived side by side in harmony, proving that kindness and courage could turn fear into friendship. And Elara, the girl who dared to see the good in the giant, was remembered as the one who changed her people’s hearts forever.
